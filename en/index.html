I am a doctoral researcher in Environmental Protection at Universiti Malaya, with prior degrees in Computer Science (B.Sc.) and Data Management and Analytics (M.IT) from The University of Sydney and software engineering experience at Huawei Technologies. My research asks how climate extremes—from tropical urban heatwaves to global dryland hydroclimatic stress—affect human populations and ecosystems, and how multi-source remote sensing and machine learning can support rigorous early warning and attribution.

During my doctoral studies, I have authored or co-authored 10 peer-reviewed articles (7 as first author, 8 in JCR Q1 venues), led by a flagship first- and corresponding-author study accepted in npj Climate and Atmospheric Science (Nature Portfolio, JCR Q1) alongside first-author papers in Journal of Environmental Management and Scientific Reports. I serve as an Expert Reviewer for Chapter 4 of the IPCC Seventh Assessment Report (AR7) and peer reviewer for five international journals.

Monthly hydroclimatic associations in sampled natural dryland vegetation

View Full Monograph & Figures →

Zecheng Li*, Xuanyu Chen, Chng Saun Fong, Yijuan Shen · npj Climate and Atmospheric Science (2026, Accepted, JCR Q1)

Monthly satellite observations provide a common timescale for relating dryland greenness to climate, although the indicators contain histories of different lengths. We analyzed Enhanced Vegetation Index (EVI) anomalies from 2001–2023 in 7,332 cells selected for natural vegetation and observation quality (covering 39.0% of the land-majority dryland grid). Of the 6,125 links retained when the current month was eligible (PCMCI+), 69.6% fell in that month and 26.5% one month earlier. The antecedent-only PCMCI search retained 5,638 links, with 83.3% at lag 1. Potential evapotranspiration (PET) received the greatest statistical support in 58.1% of detected cells. Across 18 sensitivity and robustness checks—including omitting SPEI-6, restricting to active EVI months, substituting NDVI/DEF/VPD/Tmax, fire-month exclusion, and nonlinear GPDC testing—the selected month remained substantially more consistent (92.2%–98.5% agreement) than the water-versus-demand category classification.
